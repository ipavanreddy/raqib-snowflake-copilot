## 2. Problem Framing

### 2.1 Personas

#### FCC Analyst (Financial Crime Compliance)
Top 5 questions:
1. Which alerts should I work first (highest risk, oldest open)?
2. Why did this alert fire — what specific transactions triggered the rule?
3. What does the customer's profile say versus what the transactions show?
4. Is this counterparty on a sanctions list or linked to high-risk jurisdictions?
5. Should I close this as a false positive or escalate to a case?

Manual pain today: Swivel-chair between core banking, spreadsheets of alerts, and PDF policy manuals. Each alert requires 30–45 minutes of manual evidence gathering. No single view links transactions → counterparties → KYC → policy.

#### MLRO (Money Laundering Reporting Officer)
Top 5 questions:
1. Which cases need my decision today (SLA-critical)?
2. Is this draft STR complete, accurate, and compliant (no tipping-off, amounts reconciled)?
3. What are the monthly MI numbers — alert volumes, conversion rates, backlog, ageing?
4. Are our detection rules still effective against the typologies we're calibrated for?
5. What is the cross-risk picture for a customer (AML + credit + operational)?

Manual pain today: STR narratives written from scratch in Word, with copy-paste from transaction screens. No citation trail. MI assembled monthly in Excel. MLRO has no visibility into analyst workload or queue health in real time.

#### Treasury / Risk Manager
Top 5 questions:
1. Are we above the 100% LCR regulatory minimum today? What's the buffer?
2. What drove the LCR dip this week — HQLA drawdown or outflow increase?
3. Which sectors are breaching the NPL appetite? What's the IFRS 9 stage migration?
4. Which borrowers under credit stress are also flagged by AML?
5. What is the expected credit loss (ECL) by product and sector?

Manual pain today: LCR computed daily in a spreadsheet pulling from treasury systems. Credit risk reports assembled monthly. No linkage between AML flags and credit exposure — a customer in Stage 3 and under AML investigation is discovered only by accident.

#### Audit / Model Risk
Top 5 questions:
1. Can I see the full audit trail: who asked what, which tools executed, and on what data?
2. Do the detection rules still catch all known typologies (recall test)?
3. Are false-positive rates within the agreed threshold per rule?
4. Are STR citations verifiable against actual policy passages and evidence?
5. Has any guardrail been bypassed or weakened since the last review?

Manual pain today: Audit trails are fragmented across email, case management screenshots, and analyst notes. No machine-readable log of the decision path. Rule validation is an annual manual exercise.

## 3. Design Review

### 3.1 Strengths

- **Ontology is clean and well-normalised.** The Customer→Account→Transaction→Counterparty chain with a Country risk dimension is standard and complete. The parallel AML (Alert→Case→Report) and Credit (Loan, IFRS 9) hierarchies are properly separated.
- **Semantic views as the single source of governed metrics.** Having AML_SV and RISK_SV means the same metric resolves identically in the agent, Snowsight, CoCo, and the Streamlit app — no metric drift.
- **Dynamic tables for near-real-time detection.** Avoids orchestration code and gives a natural freshness SLA (`TARGET_LAG = 5 minutes`).
- **Guardrail layer is well-thought-out.** Amount reconciliation, citation verification, tipping-off filter, and MLRO-only filing are concrete controls, not just policy statements.
- **Ground truth with planted cases.** 42 scenarios across all typologies enables deterministic recall testing — essential for both development and audit.

### 3.2 Critique and improvements

| Area | Issue | Proposed improvement |
|---|---|---|
| **Alert lifecycle** | No `ALERTS` table with mutable status; the current dynamic table is insert-only rule hits. | Add `RAQIB.CASE.ALERT_STATUS` table (ALERT_ID, STATUS, ASSIGNED_TO, STATUS_TS, RATIONALE) joined to the dynamic alerts. This supports the NEW→IN_REVIEW→ESCALATED→CLOSED lifecycle from the rulebook. |
| **TXN_ENRICHED dependency** | All 8 rules depend on `RAQIB.CORE.TXN_ENRICHED` but it's defined in `sql/03_*`. If that DT has a refresh failure, all detection goes stale silently. | Add a freshness health check (e.g., a 30-min SLA assertion on `TXN_ENRICHED`'s last refresh) and surface it in the Streamlit dashboard. |
| **Cross-risk linkage** | The solution design mentions "which borrowers are also under AML review" but there is no explicit join path between `LOANS` and `ALERTS`. | Create a `RAQIB.CORE.CUSTOMER_360` view joining CUSTOMER_PROFILE, CUSTOMER_RISK, and a loan summary (stage, ECL, DPD). This becomes the single pane for cross-risk questions. |
| **LCR not in the ontology** | The LCR data (liquidity_daily) sits in RAW but isn't represented in the ontology diagram. | Add `Bank ──< LCR_Day` to the ontology and surface LCR metrics (lcr_percent, hqla_total, net_outflow) through RISK_SV. The solution design mentions this but it should be explicitly wired. |
| **Counterparty deduplication** | 11,932 counterparties but only 5,935 unique names (50% collision rate). Name variants of the same entity may cause TM-06 to miss matches or TM-03 to undercount senders. | Consider a `COUNTERPARTY_GROUP_ID` derived from fuzzy matching on name+country, used in TM-03 and TM-06. Not blocking for demo but worth a future iteration. |
| **Transaction amount skew** | Mean AED 36k but median AED 949 — heavy right-tail. CARD_POS dominates at 45% of volume. | Not a data problem (realistic retail distribution), but rules that use absolute thresholds (TM-01 at AED 45k, TM-05 at AED 100k) should be aware that the majority of transactions are orders of magnitude below the thresholds. This is correct for AML; the rules are meant to catch the tail. |
| **Dormancy baseline** | `LAST_ACTIVITY_BEFORE_WINDOW` on accounts allows dormancy detection, but only 24 unique values suggests a coarse generation. | Acceptable for demo. In production this would come from the core banking system with exact per-account dates. |

## 5. Top Risks to a Successful Demo

| # | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cortex model availability / rate limits** | Medium | High — the copilot, STR drafting, and policy search all depend on LLM calls | Use `llama3.1-70b` as primary (generally available in all regions), with a deterministic template fallback for STR drafting. Pre-test model access before the demo. Keep token counts low with targeted evidence retrieval rather than full-context dumps. |
| 2 | **Trial credit exhaustion** | Medium | High — all compute stops | Keep warehouses XSMALL with AUTO_SUSPEND = 60. Monitor credit burn with `SNOWFLAKE.ORGANIZATION_USAGE.METERING_HISTORY`. Pre-compute dynamic tables before the demo; during the demo, rely on cached results. |
| 3 | **Dynamic table refresh latency** | Low | Medium — stale alerts during live demo | Use `TARGET_LAG = 5 minutes` (already set). Ensure the DTs are fully refreshed before the demo starts. If a refresh is slow, the data is still correct — it's synthetic and won't change during the demo. |
| 4 | **Data volume too small to impress** | Low | Medium — 215k transactions may seem trivial | Frame it: "6 months of a mid-size UAE bank." The detection rules and guardrails are the value, not the data volume. If needed, the generator can scale to millions. |
| 5 | **Cortex Search (policy retrieval) cold-start** | Medium | Medium — first query to the search service may be slow or return poor results | Index the policy PDFs ahead of the demo. Run a warmup query. Have a fallback of direct chunk retrieval from the `POLICY_CHUNKS` table if search is down. |
| 6 | **Guardrail false-trigger on legitimate STR** | Low | High — a blocked STR draft during a live demo looks like a failure | Test all 42 planted cases through the full draft-validate cycle before the demo. Have a known-good case (e.g., the MULTI_TYPOLOGY_HERO) pre-tested and ready. |
| 7 | **Region mismatch** | Low | High — Cortex AI features vary by region | Confirm the trial account's region supports Cortex Search, AI functions, and the chosen LLM. AWS `us-west-2` or `us-east-1` have the broadest support. |
| 8 | **RBAC complexity during demo** | Low | Low — switching roles mid-demo is confusing | Pre-configure the Streamlit app to use `RAQIB_ANALYST` for investigation and show the MLRO approval as a separate step. Avoid live role-switching. |
