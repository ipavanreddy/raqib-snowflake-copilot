# Gulf Horizon Bank — AML/CFT Policy

> Synthetic document created for the Raqib hackathon demo. Gulf Horizon Bank is fictional. The text is illustrative and modelled on widely published AML/CFT concepts (FATF Recommendations, UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 20 of 2018 and its implementing regulations). It is not legal advice and is not an official regulatory text.

## 1. Purpose
This policy sets the minimum standards Gulf Horizon Bank ("the Bank") applies to prevent its products and services from being used for money laundering (ML), terrorist financing (TF) or proliferation financing (PF). It applies to all branches, subsidiaries, employees and outsourced service providers.

## 2. Regulatory basis
The Bank operates under the UAE AML/CFT framework, including Federal Decree-Law No. 20 of 2018 on Anti-Money Laundering and Combating the Financing of Terrorism and Illegal Organisations, its implementing Cabinet Decision, and guidance issued by the Central Bank of the UAE. The Bank also follows the FATF 40 Recommendations as the international standard.

## 3. Governance
3.1 The Board approves this policy annually and receives a quarterly AML/CFT risk report.
3.2 The MLRO is independent of the business, reports directly to the Board Risk Committee and has the sole authority to decide whether a Suspicious Transaction Report (STR) is filed.
3.3 The first line (business) owns customer risk. The second line (Compliance) sets standards, monitors and investigates. The third line (Internal Audit) provides independent assurance.

## 4. Risk-based approach
4.1 The Bank performs an enterprise-wide ML/TF risk assessment at least annually, covering customers, products, channels, and geographies.
4.2 Every customer is assigned a risk rating of LOW, MEDIUM or HIGH at onboarding and at each review (see CDD Standard POL-CDD-004).
4.3 Higher-risk factors include: politically exposed persons (PEPs); cash-intensive businesses; dealers in precious metals and stones; real-estate brokers; money exchange houses; general trading companies with cross-border flows; and nexus to high-risk jurisdictions.

## 5. Customer due diligence
5.1 No account may be opened without completed identification and verification of the customer and beneficial owners.
5.2 Enhanced due diligence (EDD) is mandatory for HIGH-risk customers, including source-of-funds and source-of-wealth evidence and senior management approval.

## 6. Ongoing monitoring
6.1 All transactions are subject to automated transaction monitoring under the Transaction Monitoring Rulebook (POL-TM-002).
6.2 Alerts must be triaged within 2 business days and investigations completed within 10 business days of alert creation (30 days for complex cases with MLRO extension).

## 7. Red flags and typologies
7.1 Staff must be alert to activity that is inconsistent with the customer's known profile, has no apparent economic purpose, or appears designed to avoid reporting thresholds.
7.2 **Cash structuring.** The Bank applies an internal cash reporting threshold of **AED 55,000** per transaction. Breaking cash deposits into amounts just below this threshold, across days or branches, is a primary structuring indicator and must be escalated even if each deposit individually appears routine.
7.3 **Layering and pass-through.** Accounts that receive large credits and promptly forward most of the funds to multiple beneficiaries, especially across borders, may be acting as conduits. Pass-through ratios of 90% or more within 48 hours require investigation.
7.4 **Money mules.** Newly opened personal accounts receiving funds from many unrelated individuals, followed by rapid onward transfer or cash withdrawal, indicate possible mule activity (see Fraud Typologies Circular POL-FRD-007).
7.5 **Geographic risk.** Transactions involving jurisdictions subject to a FATF call for action are prohibited unless specifically authorised by the MLRO. Jurisdictions under increased monitoring require EDD.
7.6 **Trade-based money laundering.** Over- or under-invoicing, round-tripping, and payments to third parties unrelated to the underlying trade.

## 8. Suspicious transaction reporting
8.1 Any employee who knows, suspects, or has reasonable grounds to suspect ML/TF must report internally to the MLRO without delay.
8.2 The MLRO files STRs with the UAE Financial Intelligence Unit through the goAML platform without delay once suspicion is formed (see STR Filing Guide POL-STR-008).
8.3 **Tipping-off is prohibited.** No employee may disclose to the customer or any third party that an STR has been or may be filed, or that an investigation is under way. Customer communications during an investigation must be approved by Compliance.
8.4 Filing an STR in good faith does not breach any confidentiality obligation.

## 9. Sanctions and targeted financial sanctions
9.1 The Bank screens customers, counterparties, and payments against applicable sanctions lists (see Sanctions Screening Procedure POL-SAN-003).
9.2 Where a confirmed match to a targeted financial sanctions list is identified, funds must be frozen without delay and the competent authority notified.

## 10. Record keeping
All CDD records, transaction records, investigation files and STRs are retained for at least **five years** from the end of the relationship or the date of the transaction, whichever is later, and must be retrievable promptly for regulators.

## 11. Training
All staff complete AML/CFT training at onboarding and annually. Compliance and investigation staff complete advanced typology training twice a year.

## 12. Breaches
Breaches of this policy may result in disciplinary action and may constitute a criminal offence under UAE law.
